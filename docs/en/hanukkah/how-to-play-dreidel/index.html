How to Play Dreidel: Simple Rules for Kids

Updated: November 17, 2026

Dreidel is the classic Hanukkah game: a four-sided spinning top and a pot of treats. It takes a minute to learn and works for ages 4 to 94.

What you need

  • A dreidel (or print one and fold it)
  • 10–15 small pieces per player: chocolate gelt, pennies, raisins, nuts or candy
  • 2 or more players

The letters

Each side has a Hebrew letter. Together they stand for Nes Gadol Hayah Sham, “a great miracle happened there”. In Israel the last letter is Pe (Poh, “here”) instead of Shin.

LetterNameWhat you do
נNunNothing. Next player.
גGimelTake the whole pot!
הHeiTake half the pot (round down).
ש / פShin / PePut one piece in the pot.

How to play

  1. Everyone starts with the same number of pieces.
  2. Each player puts one piece in the pot.
  3. Take turns spinning; do what the letter says.
  4. Whenever the pot is empty (after a Gimel), everyone puts in one piece again.
  5. If you run out of pieces you’re out — or agree on a “loan” so little ones can keep playing.
  6. Play until one person has everything, or set a timer and whoever has the most wins.

Tips for playing with kids

  • Keep score on paper for school-age kids — it’s sneaky addition and subtraction practice.
  • For preschoolers skip the “half” rule: Hei = take two.
  • Spin on a hard, flat surface like a tray or table, not a tablecloth.

FAQ

What do the letters on a dreidel mean?

Nun, Gimel, Hei, Shin stand for “Nes Gadol Hayah Sham” — a great miracle happened there. Israeli dreidels use Pe for “Poh” — here.

What does Hei mean in dreidel?

Take half of the pot. If there’s an odd number of pieces, round down (some families round up — agree before you start).
