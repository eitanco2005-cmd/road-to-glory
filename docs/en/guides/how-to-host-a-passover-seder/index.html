How to host a Passover seder (a first-time host's guide)

Updated 2026-11-17

Hosting your first seder is less daunting with a plan. This guide covers the timeline, the seder plate, how much matzah and wine to buy, a simple menu, and ways to keep kids engaged.

When is Passover 2027?

Passover 2027 begins at sundown on Wednesday, April 21, 2027. The first seder is that evening, after nightfall. Outside Israel, most communities hold a second seder on Thursday evening, April 22; in Israel, only one seder is held. The holiday runs through April 29 outside Israel (Chabad.org; see also My Jewish Learning’s Passover overview). Many Reform Jews and Israelis observe seven days and one seder; plenty of families do one seder at home and attend a community seder the second night.

A weekday seder means guests will arrive after work, so build your timeline around a start time that is realistic for them — and consider doing most cooking on the weekend before.

Your hosting timeline

4–6 weeks before

  • Decide on one seder or two, set your guest list, and invite people.
  • Choose a haggadah and make sure you have enough copies (one per person or one per pair).
  • Ask about allergies, kids’ ages, and anyone who keeps kosher or avoids kitniyot (legumes, rice and corn, avoided by many Ashkenazi Jews; many Sephardic Jews eat them, and the Conservative movement’s law committee permitted them in 2015).
  • Order shmurah matzah or specialty items early; supplies can run short close to the holiday.

1–2 weeks before

  • Plan your menu and shopping list by headcount.
  • If you are kashering your kitchen or switching to Passover dishes, schedule that work — how far you go is a personal and communal decision; your rabbi can help.
  • Assign roles: who reads, who asks the Four Questions, who hides the afikoman.
  • Cook and freeze make-ahead dishes like soup, matzah balls and brisket.

The night before

  • Many households search for chametz by candlelight (bedikat chametz) the night before Passover begins.
  • Set the table, prepare charoset, roast the egg and shank bone, and chill the wine.

Seder day

  • Finish cooking and assemble the seder plate, salt water, matzah cover and Elijah’s cup.
  • Put pillows on chairs for reclining, and a haggadah at each seat.
  • Light holiday candles at the proper time for your location.

The seder plate

Each seder plate holds six symbolic foods. Arrangements differ between traditions, and many families add newer symbols (an orange, an olive) alongside the traditional six. Next to the plate you will need three whole matzot, covered, and a bowl of salt water. Chabad.org’s step-by-step seder guide shows one traditional layout.

How much matzah and wine to buy

Chabad.org’s seder shopping list gives concrete guidance that is easy to scale. The quantities below are per seder — double them if you are hosting two nights. Halachic minimum portions are larger than many people expect, so if you are following a traditional practice, read Chabad’s explanation or ask your rabbi.

ItemHow much (per seder)Source
Matzah3 whole matzot for each seder plate, plus about 2 for every other participant, per seder; extra for the mealChabad.org
Wine or grape juiceEnough for four cups per person per seder plus Elijah’s cup — roughly a bottle per adult per seder is a safe estimateChabad.org
Maror and chazeretA portion of bitter herbs per person for maror and again for korech; buy generouslyChabad.org
Eggs1 for each seder plate, plus extra if you serve egg in salt water as a first courseChabad.org

Example: a seder with 1 seder plate and 10 people needs about 3 + (9 × 2) = 21 matzot for the rituals, plus more to eat with the meal. Chabad notes that handmade shmurah matzah usually comes about 6–8 pieces per pound, which helps when ordering by weight. In its seder FAQ, Chabad also suggests about a bottle of wine per person per seder as a safe bet, and says grape juice is acceptable according to many opinions.

Use a seder shopping calculator to multiply these quantities by your guest count and number of seders, then add the menu items.

Menu ideas that work for a first-timer

Keep it simple: the seder itself is long, and people arrive hungry. A classic Ashkenazi-style menu might be:

  • Starter: gefilte fish with horseradish, or hard-boiled eggs in salt water
  • Soup: chicken soup with matzah balls (both freeze well)
  • Main: brisket or roast chicken; a vegetarian option such as a vegetable or mushroom matzah lasagna
  • Sides: roasted potatoes, tzimmes (carrots and sweet potatoes), a big green salad
  • Dessert: flourless chocolate cake, macaroons, fresh fruit

Sephardic and Mizrahi families often serve different dishes — lamb, rice (where kitniyot are eaten), stuffed vegetables, date-based charoset. If you are inviting guests from a different background, ask what Passover tastes like in their family; it is a lovely way to include them.

Choosing a haggadah and involving kids

The haggadah sets the tone. Options range from traditional Hebrew–English texts to shorter, contemporary, egalitarian, or kid-focused versions; ReformJudaism.org’s seder hosting checklist is a good starting point. For a first seder, choose one with clear English instructions and transliteration so everyone can follow along. Mixing copies of different haggadot can get confusing, so try to use the same edition for everyone.

Ways to keep kids engaged:

  • Let the youngest ask the Four Questions — practice in advance.
  • Hand out plague props or finger puppets for the Ten Plagues.
  • Hide the afikoman and offer a small prize for finding it.
  • Give older kids a reading or a question to pose to the table.
  • Serve a vegetable-heavy karpas course so no one is starving during the storytelling.
Customs about kitniyot, kashering, how much to read, and even which foods go on the plate differ between Orthodox, Conservative, Reform and Reconstructionist households, and between Ashkenazi and Sephardic families. When in doubt, ask your rabbi — and remember that a warm, welcoming seder is the goal.

Questions

When is the first seder in 2027?

The first seder is on the evening of Wednesday, April 21, 2027, when Passover begins at sundown. Outside Israel, many families hold a second seder on Thursday evening, April 22.

How much matzah do I need for a seder?

Chabad.org suggests three whole matzot for each seder plate plus about two for every other participant, per seder, with extra for the meal. For 10 people and one plate, that is about 21 matzot per seder.

How much wine do I need for a Passover seder?

Plan for four cups per person per seder plus Elijah's cup; Chabad.org calls about one bottle per person per seder a safe estimate. Grape juice is acceptable according to many opinions.

Do I have to hold two seders?

Outside Israel, traditional practice is two seders, while in Israel and in many Reform households one seder is held. Ask your rabbi what fits your community's practice.
