Bat mitzvah budget breakdown: three sample budgets

Updated 2026-11-18

Where does the money actually go? Here are three worked bat mitzvah budgets, from a kiddush lunch to a big evening party, with the share each category takes, so you can see what moves the total.

A word on numbers first. There is no national survey of bar and bat mitzvah spending. Published “averages” come from planners and vendors; a 2025 Philadelphia Inquirer report quoted a local planner putting even low-key celebrations at roughly $10,000–$50,000, and vendor sites like The Bash quote similar ranges. The examples below are not averages. They are worked examples built with the starting assumptions of our free bar/bat mitzvah budget calculator (average-cost US area unless noted), so you can reproduce them and swap in your own quotes.

80 adults and 20 kids, a kiddush lunch at about $25 a head, a photographer (half-day), simple flowers, $900 for outfits.

What the three budgets show

  • Food is the biggest line once there’s a party (30–40%), and it scales with every guest. Cutting 20 adults from Example 3 saves more than dropping the photo booth.
  • The format matters more than the details. Going from kiddush to kiddush-plus-party quadruples the budget in these examples.
  • Region is a multiplier. The same party in a high-cost metro runs roughly a third more in our assumptions.
  • Keep a contingency. Around 10% covers overtime, extra guests and the things nobody budgets for.

Bat-mitzvah-specific lines to plan for

Most of a bat mitzvah budget is the same as a bar mitzvah. These are the lines families say they underestimated:

  • Outfits: many girls have two (service and party), plus alterations.
  • Hair and makeup for the bat mitzvah girl (and sometimes family) on the day.
  • Candle-lighting ceremony: candles, a stand or cake, printed rhymes, the DJ’s time.
  • Photo montage or video if not included in the photo/video package.
  • Shabbat candlesticks or a meaningful gift from the family, a custom in some communities (Chabad.org).
  • Tallit, if your community’s custom is for girls to wear one.

How to build your own breakdown

  1. Decide the format (kiddush, party or both); it sets 70% of the total.
  2. Count adults and kids separately; kids’ meals usually cost less.
  3. Get two or three written per-person quotes from caterers, including tax, service charge and kosher supervision if needed.
  4. Plug the quotes into the budget calculator and adjust each line.
  5. Track actual spending against the plan as deposits go out.

Questions

How much does a bat mitzvah cost?

It ranges from a few thousand dollars for a kiddush lunch to tens of thousands for an evening party. Our worked examples come to about $7,800 for a 100-guest kiddush, $31,500 for a kiddush plus party for 120, and $50,000 for a 175-guest party in a high-cost city.

What is the biggest expense of a bat mitzvah?

Food and drink, once there is a party: about 30-40% of the total in our examples. With a kiddush only, synagogue fees, photos and clothing take a larger share.

How can I cut a bat mitzvah budget?

Reduce the guest count, choose a kiddush or daytime event instead of an evening party, pick a Sunday or off-peak date, and get competing per-person catering quotes.

Is a bat mitzvah budget different from a bar mitzvah budget?

Mostly no. The extra lines families mention are a second outfit, hair and makeup, and the candle-lighting ceremony.
