Hanukkah party planning guide

Updated 2026-11-17

Hanukkah 2026 starts on a Friday night, which makes timing a little trickier than usual. Here is how to plan a warm, low-stress party: candles, latkes, dreidel, gifts and a simple timeline.

Hanukkah 2026 dates and the Friday-night rule

Hanukkah 2026 begins at sundown on Friday, December 4, 2026, with the first candle lit that evening, and the eighth candle is lit on Friday, December 11; the holiday ends at nightfall on Saturday, December 12 (Aish). That means both the first and the eighth nights fall on Friday, which affects party timing:

  • On Friday, the menorah is lit before the Shabbat candles, while it is still light out (My Jewish Learning). Chabad.org notes the lights must keep burning until half an hour after dark — roughly an hour and a half on Friday — so standard 30-minute candles are not enough (Chabad.org).
  • On Saturday night (December 5), the menorah is lit after Shabbat ends. Communities differ on whether Havdalah or the menorah comes first.
  • Families who observe Shabbat won’t play dreidel for money, open gifts or fry latkes after candle-lighting on Friday night, so a Sunday afternoon party (December 6) is often easier.

Menorah lighting basics

On the first night you light one candle plus the shamash (helper candle); each night adds one more, until all eight burn on the last night. Candles are placed in the menorah from right to left and lit from left to right, newest first. Three blessings are said the first night and two on the following nights; My Jewish Learning has the text and transliteration.

How many candles do you need? Add up the nightly totals including the shamash: 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6 + 7 + 8 + 9 = 44 candles per menorah, which is why a standard box holds 44 (My Jewish Learning). If several people light their own menorah, buy a box for each, plus a spare for breakage.

Safety: place menorahs on a heat-proof tray away from curtains and coats, keep kids at a safe distance, and never leave lit candles unattended.

Food: latkes, sufganiyot and the rest of the table

Fried foods recall the miracle of the oil, so latkes (potato pancakes) and sufganiyot (jelly doughnuts) are the stars. Practical tips:

  • Make latkes ahead. Fry earlier in the day, cool on a rack, and reheat in a single layer in a hot oven so they crisp up again. Latkes also freeze well.
  • Toppings bar: applesauce and sour cream are the classics (the old debate is half the fun), plus smoked salmon, chives, or a spicy yogurt.
  • Buy the sufganiyot from a bakery unless you love deep-frying; order early for weekend pickup.
  • Round it out with a big salad, a brisket or a dairy main (many families serve cheese dishes, recalling the story of Judith), and fruit.
  • Kosher guests: decide on meat or dairy for the whole menu, and keep sour cream off a meat table.

Dreidel and gelt

The dreidel is a four-sided top. Its Hebrew letters — nun, gimel, hey, shin — stand for nes gadol hayah sham, “a great miracle happened there” (in Israel, the shin becomes a pe for “here”). Rules vary by family, but the common version, per My Jewish Learning, is:

  1. Everyone starts with the same number of tokens (gelt, nuts, candy) and puts one into the pot.
  2. Nun: nothing happens. Gimel: take the whole pot. Hey: take half the pot (families differ on rounding). Shin: add one token.
  3. When the pot is empty or down to one, everyone antes again. Play until one person has everything, or until the timer runs out.

Gelt — chocolate coins for kids or small amounts of real money — is a long-standing Hanukkah custom. Many families also use the occasion to talk about tzedakah, setting aside a portion of gelt for charity.

Gift ideas

Gift-giving is a newer, largely American add-on to Hanukkah, and every family handles it differently. Some ideas that fit the spirit of the holiday:

  • A child’s own menorah or a handmade dreidel
  • Jewish-themed books for kids or adults
  • A cooking gift: a cast-iron pan, a great grater, a latke kit
  • Experiences: a class, tickets, a family outing on one of the eight nights
  • A “tzedakah night,” where kids choose a cause to support instead of receiving a gift
  • For a party: a white-elephant gift swap with a price cap so no one feels pressure

Questions

When does Hanukkah start in 2026?

Hanukkah 2026 begins at sundown on Friday, December 4, with the eighth candle lit on Friday, December 11. The holiday ends at nightfall on Saturday, December 12.

How many candles do I need for Hanukkah?

One menorah needs 44 candles for all eight nights, including the shamash (2 + 3 + ... + 9). Buy a box for each menorah you light.

Do you light Hanukkah candles before or after Shabbat candles?

On Friday, the Hanukkah menorah is lit first, before the Shabbat candles and before sunset. Use candles large enough to keep burning past nightfall.

What are the rules of dreidel?

Each player antes into the pot. Nun means nothing, gimel takes the pot, hey takes half, and shin adds one; families vary on details like rounding.
