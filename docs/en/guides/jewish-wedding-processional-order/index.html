Jewish wedding processional order: who walks, who stands where

Updated 2026-11-18

A Jewish processional looks different from the one most American guests know: both partners are escorted, usually by both parents, and the parents stay under the chuppah. Here is the common order, the variations, and a printable line-up sheet.

There is no single required order: customs vary between Ashkenazi and Sephardi families, between Orthodox, Conservative and Reform communities, and between families. The order below is the one most American Jewish weddings use, as described by The Knot and My Jewish Learning. Confirm it with your rabbi, who usually runs the rehearsal.

Before the processional

Many couples hold a ketubah signing with the witnesses and a bedeken (veiling), and in more traditional weddings a tisch for the groom, before the ceremony. Guests are seated by then. In many Jewish weddings, the bride’s family and friends sit on the right side (facing the chuppah) and the groom’s on the left, the reverse of a typical Christian ceremony (The Knot). Plenty of couples skip sides altogether.

The processional, in order

  1. Rabbi and/or cantor take their places under the chuppah (or walk in first).
  2. Grandparents of the bride, then grandparents of the groom; they are seated in the front row. Some families reverse this order so the groom’s side goes first throughout.
  3. Groomsmen, singly or in pairs, then the best man.
  4. The groom, escorted by his parents, one on each side. They walk to the chuppah and the parents stand under or beside it.
  5. Bridesmaids, then the maid or matron of honor.
  6. Ring bearer and flower girl.
  7. The bride, escorted by her parents, one on each side. The groom may step forward to meet her before she enters the chuppah.

For two grooms or two brides, the same pattern works: each partner is escorted by their parents, and the couple decides who walks first.

Who stands under the chuppah?

Under the canopy: the couple, the officiant(s) and, in most families, both sets of parents. In the most common arrangement the bride stands to the groom’s right, with each set of parents on their child’s side. Grandparents usually sit in the front row. The wedding party stands beside or behind the chuppah, or sits. Four friends or relatives may hold the poles if the chuppah is hand-held, which is a lovely honor to give (ReformJudaism.org).

The recessional

After the glass is broken and the guests shout “mazal tov”, the order reverses:

  1. The couple (often straight to yichud, a few minutes alone together).
  2. The bride’s parents, then the groom’s parents.
  3. Grandparents.
  4. Flower girl and ring bearer.
  5. Wedding party, in pairs.
  6. Rabbi and/or cantor.

Questions

Who walks first in a Jewish wedding processional?

Usually the rabbi and/or cantor, followed by the grandparents, the wedding party, the groom with his parents, the bridesmaids and flower children, and finally the bride with her parents.

Do both parents walk the bride down the aisle at a Jewish wedding?

In most Jewish weddings, yes: each partner is escorted by both parents, who then stand under the chuppah. In some traditional weddings the two fathers escort the groom and the two mothers escort the bride.

Which side does the bride's family sit on at a Jewish wedding?

Often the right side facing the chuppah, the reverse of a typical Christian ceremony. Many couples don't assign sides at all.

Who stands under the chuppah?

The couple, the officiant and usually both sets of parents. Grandparents sit in the front row and the wedding party stands nearby.
