Bar and bat mitzvah planning checklist (printable)

Updated 2026-11-18

Tick-box checklist from twelve months out to the last thank-you note. Print it, stick it on the fridge, and cross things off. For the reasoning behind each step, see our month-by-month planning timeline.

Every synagogue runs its own b’nai mitzvah program. Where your synagogue’s dates differ from this list, follow the synagogue. Sequences here follow common guidance from My Jewish Learning, The Bash and New York Family; for the why behind each step, read the planning timeline.

12+ months before

  • Date confirmed in writing by the synagogue (Torah portion noted).
  • B’nai mitzvah program requirements, classes and fees understood.
  • Celebration format chosen: kiddush, evening party, brunch, trip or a mix.
  • Budget set (try our budget calculator).
  • Draft guest list with separate counts for adults and kids.
  • Venue and caterer booked (if holding a party); kosher requirements checked.
  • Hotel block considered for out-of-town guests.

9–12 months before

  • Tutoring started (per synagogue schedule).
  • Mitzvah project chosen.
  • Photographer / videographer booked.
  • DJ, band or entertainment booked.
  • Save-the-dates sent to travelers.

6–9 months before

  • Decor, florist and theme decided.
  • Invitation design chosen (print or digital).
  • Tallit, kippah and other ritual items ordered.
  • Kippot, favors or swag ordered.
  • Photo montage: start collecting photos.

3–6 months before

  • Honors list: aliyot, ark opening, other synagogue honors (ask who is eligible).
  • Candle-lighting list drafted (often about 13 candles).
  • Outfits chosen; fittings booked.
  • Kiddush or oneg sponsorship arranged with the synagogue.
  • Transportation for guests or kids arranged, if needed.

6–8 weeks before

  • Invitations mailed (earlier for travelers).
  • RSVP deadline set 3–4 weeks before the event.
  • D’var Torah draft reviewed with the rabbi or tutor.
  • Menu tasting and final menu.

3–4 weeks before

  • Missing RSVPs chased.
  • Final headcount (adults / kids) sent to the caterer.
  • Seating chart done (see bar mitzvah seating).
  • Hebrew names of aliyah honorees collected for the rabbi.
  • Candle-lighting order and music sent to the DJ.

1–2 weeks before

  • Rehearsal in the sanctuary.
  • Vendors confirmed: arrival times, contacts, final payments.
  • Tip envelopes prepared.
  • Escort cards and table numbers printed.
  • Welcome bags for hotel guests packed.

The day before

  • Speech printed (two copies).
  • Tallit, kippah, clothes laid out.
  • Day-of kit: safety pins, stain remover, snacks, phone chargers, pen for the guest book.
  • Decor and printed items delivered to the venue (before Shabbat, if relevant).

The day of: service

  • Arrive early; check in with the rabbi and gabbai.
  • Honorees know their cue and have the blessings text.
  • Someone assigned to greet guests and hand out kippot.
  • Photos per synagogue rules (many don’t allow photography on Shabbat).

The day of: party

  • One person (not a parent) is the vendor contact.
  • Candle-lighting honorees seated near the front.
  • Gift box or table watched by a trusted person.
  • Final payments and tips handed out.

After

  • Every gift logged (name, gift, address).
  • Thank-you notes written within a few weeks.
  • Tzedakah from gift money decided with your child.
  • Thank-yous to the tutor, rabbi and synagogue staff.
  • Photos backed up; reviews left for vendors.

Who owns what

Checklists stall when everything sits with one parent. A split that works for many families:

Who owns which tasks
WhoOwns
Bar/bat mitzvahTorah and haftarah study, d’var Torah, mitzvah project, thank-you notes, candle-lighting rhymes (with help)
Parent 1Synagogue contact, honors list, Hebrew names, tutor schedule, invitations and RSVPs
Parent 2Venue, caterer, vendors, budget and payments, seating chart
A grandparent or friendOut-of-town guests: hotel block, welcome bags, rides
Day-of point person (not a parent)Vendor arrivals, timeline, tips, gift table

Put names next to each tick box on your printed copy, and review the list together once a month; ten minutes is enough.

Questions

When should I start planning a bar mitzvah?

About 12 to 18 months before, once the synagogue confirms the date. Busy venues and vendors in big cities can book out a year or more ahead.

When do bar mitzvah invitations go out?

Commonly 6 to 8 weeks before, with save-the-dates months earlier for guests who travel. Set the RSVP deadline 3 to 4 weeks before the event.

What goes in a day-of kit?

Two copies of the speech, the tallit and kippah, safety pins, stain remover, snacks, phone chargers, tip envelopes and a pen for the guest book.

What is the difference between this checklist and the planning timeline?

The timeline explains each step; this page is the short tick-box version designed to print and carry around.
