Why multiples of 18?

In Hebrew, letters double as numbers (gematria). The word chai, "life", is spelled chet (8) and yud (10), which add up to 18. Giving in multiples of 18 is a way of wishing the bar or bat mitzvah a long, good life, and the same custom is common for tzedakah (charity) donations. As ReformJudaism.org puts it, it's a symbolic custom, not a requirement.

How people decide the amount

  • Closeness comes first. Family and close friends give more than classmates or coworkers.
  • A couple or a family usually gives one larger gift rather than one gift each.
  • You don't have to "cover your plate". Some people think about what the party costs per guest, but most etiquette guides say a bar mitzvah gift is about the child, not the catering bill.
  • Teens giving their own gift typically give $18–$54. A thoughtful card matters more than the amount.
  • Can't attend? Sending a gift is still a kind gesture, often a little less than if you were attending.

Check, cash or Venmo?

Checks made out to the bar or bat mitzvah (or a parent) are traditional and easy to track. Cash in a card works, and many families are fine with Zelle or Venmo now. Some guests give Israel Bonds, a contribution to the child's mitzvah project, or a donation in their honor. Bring the card to the party, or mail it if you're not attending.

Questions

Is a bat mitzvah gift the same as a bar mitzvah gift?

Yes. The same customs and amounts apply to bar and bat mitzvahs (and b'nai mitzvah).

Do I have to give a multiple of 18?

No. It's a meaningful custom, not a rule. A gift of $50 or $100 is perfectly fine; many people simply round to the nearest chai amount like $54 or $108.

How much should a 13-year-old give to a classmate?

Teens giving their own gift usually give $18 to $54, or a small present. Parents sometimes add to it for a very close friend.

Should I give more if the party is fancy?

Most etiquette guides say no: the gift celebrates the child, it isn't payment for the meal. Give according to your relationship and what you can afford.

What if I'm not attending?

It's still kind to send a card and gift. Many people give a little less than they would if attending.
