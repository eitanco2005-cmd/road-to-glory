Wedding seating chart template: the three lists you actually need

Updated 2026-11-18

A seating chart is really three lists: a guest list with the right columns, a table plan, and an alphabetical escort list for the welcome table. Here is a template for each, with a worked example you can copy.

Most “seating chart templates” online are a pretty poster with empty circles. The poster is the last step. What saves you the evening is the data behind it: who is coming, how many seats each invitation takes, and which group they belong to. Get that right and the circles fill themselves in.

Part 1: the guest list (one row per invitation)

Start from your RSVP list, not your invitation list. Put one row per invitation (a couple or family is one row) and count seats, so a family of four never gets split by accident.

Tip: write the group as specifically as you can. “Friends” is useless when you have 60 of them; “Partner A’s soccer team” is a table.

Part 2: the table plan

Before you seat anyone, get the floor plan and the table sizes from your venue. As a rule of thumb, rental companies list a 60-inch round at 8–10 guests and a 72-inch round at 10–12; most planners treat 8 and 10 as the comfortable numbers (see Chiavari Chair Sales and Party Rental Ltd.). Then work table by table:

That example seats 120 guests at twelve tables of ten plus a spare. If your venue uses 60-inch rounds and you want room to breathe, plan 15 tables of 8 instead. The math is simple: guests ÷ seats per table, round up, add one.

Part 3: the escort list (A–Z)

Guests look for their own name, not for a table. So the list at the entrance is sorted by last name, one line per invitation, with the table number. This is the list your stationer prints on escort cards or a big display board, and the one the venue coordinator keeps in their pocket.

Escort cards vs. place cards: escort cards tell guests which table; place cards tell them which seat. Most weddings only need escort cards. Use place cards if you are serving plated meals with different choices, or at the head table.

Before you print: a 10-minute check

  • Every confirmed guest has a table (no one left in “unseated”).
  • No table is over capacity, counting kids and high chairs.
  • Nobody is at a table with someone on their keep-apart list.
  • Older guests and anyone using a wheelchair are near an aisle and not next to the speakers.
  • Each table has at least one person who knows most of the others.
  • Meal counts per table match what you sent the caterer.
  • The spare table exists and is on the floor plan.
  • The escort list is alphabetical by last name and matches the table plan.

Questions

What should a wedding seating chart template include?

A guest list with one row per invitation (name, number of seats, side, group, needs, keep-apart, meal), a table plan with capacity per table, and an alphabetical escort list with each party's table number.

How many guests fit at a round wedding table?

Rental companies typically list a 60-inch round at 8-10 guests and a 72-inch round at 10-12. Many planners use 8 and 10 as the comfortable numbers. Confirm with your venue, because chair and charger sizes change the answer.

Should the escort list be sorted by table or by name?

By last name. Guests look for their own name, so an alphabetical list (name, then table number) is much faster at the entrance. Keep a by-table version for the venue and caterer.

When should I make the seating chart?

Draft it once most RSVPs are in, usually 3 to 5 weeks before the wedding, and finalize it after your RSVP deadline so you can give the caterer final counts.
