Ritual amounts start from Chabad.org's per-person guidance; meal amounts are catering rules of thumb. Customs and halachic minimums vary: ask your rabbi, and round up for hearty eaters.

How much matzah do you need?

Chabad.org suggests three whole matzot for each seder plate (the leader's plate) plus about two more for every other participant, for each seder, and notes that handmade shmurah matzah is sold by weight, roughly 6–8 round matzot per pound. Machine matzah for the meal and for snacking during the week comes on top of that. Our calculator uses 2 shmurah matzot per adult and 1.5 per child per seder, plus 3 per seder plate.

Wine and grape juice

Everyone drinks four cups over the seder. Chabad.org suggests planning roughly a bottle of wine per adult per seder to be safe, less if many people use small cups. Kids (and anyone who prefers it) drink grape juice. We plan ¾ of a 750 ml bottle per adult and half a 64 oz bottle per child per seder, and add a little for Elijah's cup and the charoset.

Seder plate items

  • Maror: about 2 oz per person per night (Chabad.org), usually romaine lettuce and/or fresh horseradish.
  • Karpas: a small piece of parsley, celery or potato per person.
  • Charoset: apples, walnuts and sweet wine; about a quarter apple per person.
  • Zeroa and beitzah: one shank bone (or roasted chicken neck; many vegetarians use a beet) and one egg per plate, plus an egg per person if your family eats eggs in salt water.
