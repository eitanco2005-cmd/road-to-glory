What drives the cost

  • Guest count × catering. Food and drink for the party is usually the biggest line, and kosher catering costs more.
  • Kiddush vs evening party. A kiddush luncheon after services is far cheaper per person than a dinner party with a DJ.
  • Where you live. Big-city venues and vendors can cost much more than the same party elsewhere.
  • Entertainment. A DJ with an MC and dancers, or a live band, is often the second-biggest line.
  • Weekend and season. Popular Saturday nights book early and cost more.

Published estimates vary enormously, from a few thousand dollars for a kiddush and a family dinner to five or even six figures for big-city evening parties. Planning sites like The Bash list the guest count, the location and whether you hold a kiddush, a party or both as the main budget drivers, and a 2025 Philadelphia Inquirer report noted that some lavish parties top $100,000 while many families plan far smaller events. Treat any single "average" with care.

Ways to spend less

  • Hold a Shabbat kiddush luncheon at the synagogue, plus a casual kids' party instead of a formal dinner.
  • Choose a Sunday brunch or an off-season date.
  • Trim the guest list first: every guest adds catering, seats, décor and favors.
  • Use digital invitations and RSVPs.
  • Put part of the budget toward a trip to Israel or the child's savings instead.
