# Traffic, not product, decides the first $1,000

Under the current constraints, no path in the research makes $1,000 profit within 30 to 60 days realistic. My estimate is **about 1 to 2% by day 30, 2 to 4% by day 60, and 5 to 8% by day 90**. These figures are judgment, built from the sourced base rates below, not measured. The binding constraint is **distribution**. The owner rules out outreach, social media and new accounts, and that leaves organic search on a brand-new free subdomain plus GitHub as the only ways buyers can arrive. The evidence on both channels points to tens or low hundreds of visitors in the first 90 days. $1,000 needs roughly 50 to 100 sales at $10 to $20, which at cold-traffic conversion means about 10,000 to 100,000 visitors. The closest real-world analog is an AI-agent-run portfolio of 25 sites: **$0 revenue after 100 days** ([DEV](https://dev.to/yonatan_naor_5642e43447ea/an-ai-ran-a-web-business-for-100-days-revenue-0-herex27s-the-full-audit-483i)). Payments (PayPal only) and product quality are not what holds this back. Bounties, bug bounties and competitions do not get around the problem, because each needs a new account with KYC or a tax form. The sensible strategy is to put a small number of high-intent, pay-at-the-moment-of-need tools in front of whatever search traffic arrives, make every result shareable over WhatsApp, and treat this as a 3 to 12 month build. A sprint to $1,000 in 30 days is not available. If the owner ever wants to change the odds, the single action that would matter most is one marketplace account where buyers already search (Etsy). That is offered as information, not a request.

## Fast first sales came from marketplaces, not new websites

The documented cases of quick template revenue share one feature: the buyers were already there. Pretty Arrow sold its first budget spreadsheet on day one and now reports around $10K a month, but its early sales came **through Etsy, not its own site** ([Starter Story](https://starterstory.com/stories/pretty-arrow)). Sellers who went straight to their own site saw the slower pattern. A Google Sheets tutorials seller made one $30 sale and then only one more in the next two weeks ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/on-creating-and-monetising-google-sheets-tutorials)). A resume builder got its first $19 customer only "after months of building and no marketing" ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/first-paying-customer-for-my-resume-builder-after-months-of-building-and-no-marketing-af90b95967)).

Even platforms with built-in discovery have harsh base rates. In Gumroad's own 2020 data, the largest group of creators earned **under $100 for the year** ([Silicon Florist](https://siliconflorist.com/2021/01/12/what-happened-in-the-gumroad-creator-community-in-2020/)). Third-party estimates, with unclear methods, say only about **0.4% of Gumroad products clear $1,000** ([profitable.app](https://profitable.app/gumroad/stats)). Developer kits show the same pattern. Tailwind UI's $500K in three days rode a huge existing open-source audience ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/adam-wathan-just-made-500k-in-3-days-from-his-new-product-603feefe61)). A small Tailwind library is the more typical case: **$500 after 2.5 months** ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/zukoni-ui-library/500-in-sales-after-2-5-months-for-tailwindcss-lib--MI3KqJJS533MwIlMHzl)). All of these stories are survivors. The shops that made nothing did not publish.

The Hebrew market adds a second problem: strong free competition. WPS, Picsart and PDF24 offer **free Hebrew CV builders** with PDF export ([WPS](https://www.wps.com/he-IL/feature/resume-builder-online/); [PDF24](https://tools.pdf24.org/he/create-job-application)). mit4mit's WedPlanner includes **free seating arrangements** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/il/app/wedplanner-%D7%90%D7%A8%D7%92%D7%95%D7%9F-%D7%97%D7%AA%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%94/id1618735378)). The Ministry of Education publishes free worksheets ([meyda.education.gov.il](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/free%20books/%D7%A4%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%98%20%D7%97%D7%A9%D7%91%D7%95%D7%9F%20%D7%942.pdf)), and financa.co.il gives away wedding-budget Excel guidance ([financa](https://financa.co.il/%d7%aa%d7%9b%d7%a0%d7%95%d7%9f-%d7%aa%d7%a7%d7%a6%d7%99%d7%91-%d7%9c%d7%97%d7%aa%d7%95%d7%a0%d7%94/)). Israelis do pay where the offer feels personal or like a service. Custom invitation design costs **₪200 to ₪600** ([Midrag](https://www.midrag.co.il/Content/Tip/25878)), and the Attending RSVP app charges **about ₪200 per wedding** ([mako](https://www.mako.co.il/news-money/tech12/Article-790c1be6c65eb91027.htm)). So a Hebrew product earns its price by doing something the free tools do badly, such as correct RTL output, Israeli guest counts or reserve tables. A generic Hebrew spreadsheet does not.

## A free netlify.app subdomain gets almost no search traffic for months

With outreach ruled out, search is effectively the only traffic source, and the evidence on new sites is discouraging. Ahrefs reportedly found that only **1.74% of newly published pages reach Google's top 10 within a year** ([Authority Hacker summary](https://www.authorityhacker.com/how-long-to-rank-on-google/)). Practitioner guidance puts the first clicks at around **months 6 to 9** for small new sites ([behindrankings](https://behindrankings.com/seo-for-a-new-website/)). The subdomain makes this worse. In January 2026 Google's John Mueller said free subdomain hosting "attracts a lot of spam & low-effort content" and **makes SEO harder** even when the site does everything else right ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-mueller-free-subdomain-hosting-makes-seo-harder/565249/)). netlify.app is on the Public Suffix List, so each subdomain counts as its own site, but it still shares that neighbourhood ([Public Suffix List](https://github.com/publicsuffix/list)).

The anecdotes match. One builder made 140 SEO pages in three weeks and got **one Google click** ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-built-140-seo-pages-in-3-weeks-google-sent-me-one-click-Tpte8rPKC2689FwnsOvQ)). The 25-site AI portfolio logged 382 sessions across all its sites in 30 days ([DEV](https://dev.to/yonatan_naor_5642e43447ea/i-let-ai-agents-run-my-website-portfolio-for-30-days-heres-what-actually-happened-2ib4)). Mass AI content carries extra risk. In SE Ranking's experiment, 2,000 AI articles on 20 new domains ranked at first and then **disappeared from Google after about three months** ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/se-ranking-study-shows-ai-content-disappears-from-search-after-3-months/)). That fits Google's 2024 "scaled content abuse" policy ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/scaled-content-abuse/)). Interactive tools with real utility are less exposed than mass-produced text pages, which is one more reason to favor tools.

The other no-account channels have low ceilings. IndexNow needs no account and can get pages into Bing within hours for known sites ([IndexNow](https://www.indexnow.org/documentation); [Tygart Media](https://tygartmedia.com/indexnow-speed-test-bing-gpt-google-crawl-comparison/)). But Bing has only **about 1.1% of search in Israel** compared with Google's roughly 98%, and **about 7.5 to 8.4% in the US** ([StatCounter Israel](https://gs.statcounter.com/search-engine-market-share/all/israel); [Statista](https://statista.com/statistics/1383614/bing-search-engine-market-share-united-states)). So IndexNow helps the English pages a little and the Hebrew pages almost not at all. AI assistants sent only **0.32% of website traffic in 2026** ([SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/blog/ai-traffic-research-study/)), and llms.txt files almost never get read ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/llms-txt-adoption-rises-8-8x-but-97-of-files-get-zero-ai-requests/)). GitHub topics and READMEs are a real but small discovery surface, and README links are reported to be nofollow ([DEV](https://dev.to/chetansanghani/medium-and-github-both-went-nofollow-so-i-built-a-40-line-powershell-backlink-verifier-o41)). Their main value is getting Googlebot to the site. One lever needs no outreach at all: WhatsApp reaches about **99% of Israelis** ([CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/rkqhuhexfl)). A tool whose result is worth forwarding ("our seating plan," "my budget") can spread through its own users. No data was found on how often Israelis actually share tools this way, so treat it as a design bet, not a forecast.

## The arithmetic behind the probability estimates

The conversion side is better documented. Cold traffic to a digital product page converts at roughly **1.9%** on Gumroad, per one unverified source ([InsightRaider](https://insightraider.com/en/data/gumroad-statistics-2026)), and the average Shopify store converts at **1.4%** ([Nudgify](https://www.nudgify.com/shopify-conversion-rate-benchmarks/)). Freemium benchmarks of 3 to 8% are measured on signed-up users, not on visitors ([ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/)). Tip jars convert worst of all. One unverified claim says only about 21% of creators with tip links receive even one tip a month ([voxbooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/digital-tipping-creator-statistics-2026)). PayPal-only checkout costs something, but not much by comparison. Stripe measured about a **10% conversion lift** from adding Apple Pay ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/sessions/2024/a-look-under-stripes-hood-using-data-to-demystify-payments)), so the missing wallets matter less than missing visitors. One payment question is still open: no source confirmed that PayPal guest card checkout works for Israeli-issued cards on an Israeli merchant account.

Combining these gives a rough funnel. Visitors: about 0 to 100 by day 30, 20 to 300 by day 60, and 50 to 1,000 by day 90. Overall visitor-to-sale: about 0.1 to 0.5%. The base case is **0 to 5 sales by day 90**. $1,000 needs either a viral sharing loop or a few unusually high-intent pages that rank early, and the research gives no reason to expect either within 60 days.

| Horizon | Probability of $1,000 profit (estimate) | Main reason |
|---|---|---|
| 30 days | ~1–2% | Indexing alone eats most of the window. Hanukkah arrives about Dec 4, too soon for a new page to rank. |
| 60 days | ~2–4% | Clicks on a new free subdomain typically start later than this. |
| 90 days | ~5–8% | Long-tail tool pages may bring hundreds of visits a month. It still takes a sharing loop or a breakout page. |
| 90 days, with one Etsy shop | Clearly higher, not quantified | Marketplace search demand is the only documented route to fast first sales ([Starter Story](https://starterstory.com/stories/pretty-arrow)). |

The non-storefront options do not rescue the timeline. Algora, Opire and huntr pay through **Stripe Connect with KYC** ([Algora docs](https://docs.algora.io/bounties/payments); [Opire docs](https://docs.opire.dev/rewards/pricing)). HackerOne pays via PayPal, but only after **identity verification and a tax form** ([HackerOne](https://docs.hackerone.com/en/articles/8395720-payment-preferences)). Kaggle pays winners **about 2 to 3 months after a competition closes** ([Kaggle slides](https://www.kaggle.com/static/slides/meetkaggle.pdf)). Maintainers are also closing the door on agent-written PRs. Godot bans them, tldraw auto-closes outside PRs, and cURL shut its bug bounty because of AI-generated reports ([InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/02/ai-floods-close-projects); [Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/news/godot-gives-vibe-coders-the-middle-finger-as-it-bans-ai-generated-code-contributions/)). Chasing bounties would put the owner's one GitHub account at reputational risk for little expected money. One more uncertainty: Israeli residents are generally taxed on worldwide income. The notes flag this but do not verify it, so "no tax registration" could become a problem if revenue becomes regular, even though it is not a barrier to the first sales.

## The binding constraint, and the one owner action that matters most

The binding constraint is the **no-new-accounts / no-outreach rule as it applies to distribution**. Payments, hosting and build capacity are all adequate. The single owner action that would most change the odds is **opening one Etsy shop for the English Jewish-life planners and printables**. The fast-first-sale evidence comes from exactly that setup ([Starter Story](https://starterstory.com/stories/pretty-arrow)). Hanukkah searches peak in December ([rankhero](https://www.rankhero.com/keywords/hanukkah-festival-of-lights)), and comparable bar mitzvah planners sell for about **$9.99** ([Gumroad](https://culiner.gumroad.com/l/gsqbg)). The costs are real: a new account, small listing fees, and a stronger case for tax registration. Etsy's payout terms for Israel were not checked. A much smaller step, a few minutes if the owner already has a Google account, is verifying tichnun.netlify.app in Search Console with an HTML tag, which works on netlify.app subdomains ([Netlify Answers](https://answers.netlify.com/t/will-seo-work-for-xyz-netlify-app/72336)). It speeds up Google discovery and gives the team query data, but it improves the odds by a few points at most. Either step is the owner's choice. The plan below assumes neither.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the goal. $1,000 in 30 to 60 days is not something more products, better products or better copy can deliver under these rules, because the missing input is visitors, and none of the allowed channels can manufacture them quickly. Each additional product mostly adds maintenance while traffic stays the same, so the most valuable work is concentrating on a few tools people use at the exact moment they would pay, and making each one spread through its own users over WhatsApp. On a 6 to 12 month horizon those same assets are a reasonable bet. Treating them as a 30-day sprint sets up a predictable disappointment.

The unresolved questions are concrete and checkable. Does PayPal guest card checkout work for Israeli cards on this merchant account? Does any page produce a PayPal sale or a measurable referral by day 45? Would the owner accept a single marketplace account? The answers to those three questions will move the odds more than any further building.
