## Top 5 recommendations for the manager

1. **Make direct local outreach the main engine, not Fiverr/Upwork.** New marketplace
   sellers commonly get zero orders for 2+ weeks; the $1,000 goal depends on ~4–5 direct
   clients. Owner target: 10 personal contacts/day from day 5 (phone for trades, walk-in
   for salons), plus 20–30 one-to-one referral asks to friends & family on day 1.
2. **Niche: trades/home services first, salons/barbers second; skip restaurants as primary.**
   Trades have high job value and weak sites; salons are easy walk-ins. Ask worker A to
   make the plumber demo the hero of the portfolio and to add a "free mock-up" CTA.
3. **Pricing: keep $149/$299 for direct clients; use $79/$149/$299 on Fiverr at launch**
   (raise Basic after 3 reviews); $149/$299/$449 on Upwork Project Catalog. Add a $49
   add-on (Google Business Profile + domain setup) and an optional $15/month care plan for
   recurring revenue. Offer "pay after you approve the first version" to beat the
   no-reviews trust gap.
4. **"Free personalised mock-up" is the closing tool.** The AI team can turn a template
   into a mock-up for a specific prospect in minutes; the owner shows it on a phone or
   sends the link. Suggest the manager set up a fast loop: owner sends prospect name +
   niche + 3 details → a worker produces a mock-up page in `docs/mockups/{slug}/`
   (noindex, removed if no deal).
5. **Template pack: expect $0–300 in month 1; drive it with build-in-public posts.**
   Sales for such products come from the seller's own posts, not Gumroad Discover (30% fee).
   Launch with X + LinkedIn + IndieHackers on the same day, r/SideProject the next day,
   r/webdev only on Showoff Saturday. Check Gumroad's minimum payout (reported raised to
   $100 in 2026) — if true, consider Lemon Squeezy so cash isn't stuck.
